MVP 1 Calendar Planning and Executive Scheduling Rulebook
A.1 Purpose of This Appendix
This appendix is the operating rulebook for the MVP 1 proof of concept.
It is intended to give a human or software agent enough structured information to:
* understand how LSS builds its annual calendar;
* identify the meetings that belong on the central organizational calendar;
* understand each meeting’s purpose, cadence, timing, dependencies, and flexibility;
* generate a proposed 2027 calendar from approved rules;
* identify conflicts, missing information, and decisions requiring human input;
* support limited day-to-day executive scheduling recommendations; and
* explain why a meeting date or scheduling recommendation was proposed.
The appendix is not a final production configuration, legal interpretation, or approved 2027 calendar. The 2026 materials establish a baseline, but 2026 dates must not automatically be treated as permanent rules.
The MVP 1 agent may generate recommendations, but it must not create, move, cancel, or publish Outlook events without human approval.
________________


A.2 MVP 1 Scope
MVP 1 includes two connected capabilities.
1. Annual Calendar Planning
The agent should use approved meeting rules, holidays, dependencies, attendance needs, workload periods, and executive constraints to generate a proposed 2027 calendar.
2. Lightweight Executive Scheduling Advisor
The agent should help an Executive Assistant or executive evaluate a new meeting request by recommending:
* whether the meeting should be accepted;
* where it could fit;
* what existing meeting could move, if any;
* how a displaced meeting could be rescheduled; and
* why the recommendation follows the agreed scheduling rules.
The following are not part of MVP 1:
* autonomous calendar changes;
* email-based scheduling negotiation;
* bulk maintenance of confidential meeting instances;
* automated onboarding or offboarding;
* automated SharePoint or distribution-list updates; and
* production Outlook integration before the prototype is validated.

A.3 How the Agent Must Interpret the Rules
Rule Status Labels
Every scheduling rule should carry one of the following statuses.
Confirmed
The rule was explicitly stated in the recent discovery conversations or is consistently supported by current documents.
2026 Baseline
The rule or pattern was used in 2026 and may be used to create a POC draft, but it has not yet been approved for 2027.
Needs Validation
The rule appears likely or is supported by one source, but the team must confirm it before it is treated as authoritative.
Open Question
Required information is missing or conflicting. The agent must not invent an answer.
Source Precedence
When two sources differ, use this order:
1. Approved 2027 decisions from Rachel, Kim, the Executive Committee, or another authorized owner.
2. The most recent discovery-session statements.
3. The detailed meeting-specific calendar sheet.
4. The Meeting Matrix.
5. The observed 2026 calendar pattern.
6. Agent inference.
Agent inference must never be converted into an approved rule. It must be presented as a recommendation or open question.
Constraint Strength
Rules should also be classified by strength.
Hard Stop
The proposed date cannot be used without an explicit override.
Examples:
* an approved organizational holiday;
* an approved blackout period;
* an unavailable required attendee;
* a compliance deadline that would be missed; or
* an approved Board meeting that cannot be moved.
Required Sequence
One activity must happen before or after another.
Example:
* Finance Committee review must occur before the related Board approval.
Default Rule
Use the rule unless a valid conflict or approved exception exists.
Example:
* Leadership Team normally meets on the second Thursday.
Preference
Optimize for the rule when possible, but it may be overridden.
Examples:
* group downtown meetings together;
* avoid unnecessary executive travel; or
* limit evening commitments.

Appendix B — Annual Calendar Construction Rules
B.1 Required Build Order
The agent must build the calendar in the same order that LSS leadership thinks about the year.
Step 1 — Load the Calendar Constraints
Before proposing meetings, load:
* the official 2027 calendar;
* approved national holidays;
* approved LSS holidays;
* approved blackout periods;
* the Christmas-through-New-Year closure or no-meeting period;
* known Board-member availability constraints;
* executive PTO and calendar blocks;
* compliance deadlines;
* budget and financial-close periods;
* performance-evaluation periods;
* grant or reporting periods, when provided; and
* other high-workload periods identified by Kim, Felise, Lauren, or another process owner.
The agent must not copy holiday labels from the 2026 master sheet into 2027. A verified 2027 holiday and LSS closure list is required.
Step 2 — Build the Board Skeleton
Place the annual Board structure first:
1. Full Board meetings
2. Board retreats
3. Board Critical Issue Check-ins
4. Executive Committee meetings
5. Administration & Finance Committee meetings
6. Other Board committees
7. Board orientation or special Board events, when confirmed
Once approved, Board dates become protected anchors around which the rest of the organizational calendar is built.
Step 3 — Apply Board and Compliance Dependencies
Before adding organizational meetings, validate:
* committee-before-Board sequencing;
* financial-report availability;
* budget-review and Board-approval deadlines;
* succession-planning requirements;
* other compliance-calendar obligations; and
* whether an authoritative Board or Executive Committee body is available when decisions may be required.
Step 4 — Add Executive and Organizational Meetings
Add meetings in this order:
1. Executive Team meetings and retreats
2. Leadership Team meetings and retreats
3. Supervisory Team meetings
4. All Staff meetings
5. Quarterly Program Briefings
6. Operations meetings
7. BVR meetings
8. Internal Risk meetings
9. Other validated organizational or committee meetings
Step 5 — Apply Workload and Attendance Constraints
Check:
* budget and financial-close periods;
* performance-evaluation periods;
* reporting and program deadlines;
* common vacation periods;
* executive and required-attendee PTO;
* whether full attendance is required;
* whether representation by another leader is sufficient; and
* whether too many major meetings burden the same audience in one week.
Step 6 — Cross-Check Executive Calendars
Cross-check the proposed structure against:
* Rachel’s external Board commitments;
* major donor, funder, elected-official, and community obligations;
* Kim’s major internal organizational commitments;
* other known executive constraints; and
* approved PTO blocks.
Step 7 — Assign Times
Assign meeting times only after the annual date structure is coherent.
Step 8 — Generate the Draft and Decision Queue
The agent should produce:
* a proposed year-at-a-glance calendar;
* a meeting-level schedule;
* warnings and conflicts;
* proposed alternatives;
* an explanation for each exception;
* unresolved open questions; and
* a list of items requiring human approval.
Rachel, Kim, Felise, or another authorized leader must approve the proposed calendar before any Outlook action is taken.
The annual view should support Rachel’s preferred layout of three months across and four months down so quarterly patterns are easy to see.

B.2 Cross-Cutting Calendar Rules
Holidays and Blackout Periods
* Do not schedule meetings on approved national or LSS holidays.
* Do not schedule routine governance or organizational meetings during approved blackout periods.
* Treat the period between Christmas and New Year as unavailable unless leadership explicitly approves an exception.
* When a recurring Monday meeting lands on a Monday holiday, propose a replacement date rather than silently skipping or retaining the holiday date.
* A recurring cadence rule does not override a holiday or blackout rule.
Required Attendance
Every meeting should be assigned one of these attendance rules:
Full attendance required
The meeting should move if a required participant is unavailable.
Core attendance required
The meeting may proceed if the required role or leadership coverage is present, even if one individual is absent.
Attendance preferred
The meeting can proceed with partial attendance.
Informational
The event does not require every invited participant to attend.
Known example:
* A regular Executive Team meeting may proceed if one executive is absent.
* An Executive Team retreat should generally be planned for full attendance.
Workload Protection
The agent should identify and flag major meetings scheduled during known high-workload periods.
Known or emerging periods include:
* the period immediately before financial information is finalized;
* annual budget development and approval;
* July/August performance evaluations;
* grant or regulatory reporting periods, once provided;
* significant program deadlines; and
* common vacation periods.
Workload periods should initially be treated as preferences or warnings unless leadership identifies them as hard blackouts.
Rule Maintenance
Rules must be easy for authorized staff to update.
A rule may need:
* an effective start date;
* an effective end date;
* a temporary exception;
* a different rule for a specific executive;
* a change in cadence;
* an updated attendee group; or
* an updated flexibility level.
The agent should use the current rule version applicable to the proposed meeting date.
Exceptions
When a standard cadence produces an invalid date:
1. Preserve the meeting’s purpose and dependency.
2. Search for the nearest valid alternative.
3. Prefer changing the time before changing the date when that resolves the conflict.
4. Explain which rule caused the exception.
5. Record the approved alternative and rationale.
6. Do not convert a one-time exception into a permanent rule.

F.7 Scheduling Recommendation Process
For each new request, the advisor should:
1. Validate the request details.
2. Retrieve the current executive calendar and PTO blocks.
3. Apply the executive-specific rule profile.
4. Classify the request’s priority.
5. Identify protected commitments.
6. Search for available options that do not require displacement.
7. If displacement is necessary, consider only eligible flexible meetings.
8. Propose a replacement date for every displaced meeting.
9. Consider attendance, travel, location, workload, and evening limits.
10. Present the recommendation and at least one alternative when possible.
11. Explain which rules were applied.
12. Wait for human approval.
13. Make no Outlook change during the sandbox POC.

Appendix I — Deferred Phase 2 Reference
The following information is intentionally excluded from MVP 1 implementation.
I.1 Confidential Meeting Management
LSS currently avoids recurring Outlook series for protected meeting categories because an occasional attendee could gain access to notes, recordings, summaries, or artifacts from other occurrences.
Known operating rule:
* Create each protected occurrence as a separate Outlook event.
* Invite only the attendees authorized for that occurrence.
* Keep occurrence-specific artifacts scoped to that meeting.
* When attendance changes, update each relevant future meeting instance.
A future Phase 2 could create and maintain these separate instances in bulk while preserving the confidentiality boundary.
I.2 Role Changes and Onboarding/Offboarding
When a Board member or internal team member joins, leaves, or changes roles, updates may be required across:
* role or roster records;
* Outlook meetings;
* distribution lists;
* SharePoint access;
* PayCom or organizational groups;
* orientation;
* forms;
* training; and
* other role-specific requirements.
This applies to Board members and internal groups such as Executive Team, Leadership, Supervisory Team, and All Staff.
A future Phase 2 should be role-driven and effective-dated rather than based only on replacing one named individual with another. It is not required for the MVP 1 calendar POC.
